# Spec 122: Each industry posting records whether it can hire someone based in Türkiye, with its evidence

## Problem

The candidate works remotely from outside the EU and may contract through
a legal entity. Whether an employer can
engage someone in Türkiye is a different question from whether the role
fits them technically. The tracker has no place to hold the answer, so
the answer either goes missing or lands inside the technical score.

What happens today:

- **Eligibility has no field.** A tracker row has `fit_score`, `signals`
  and `rejection_reason`
  (`services/api/src/harrier/tracker/schema.py`, `TRACKER_FIELDS` and
  `NOTE_KEYS`). A finding such as "the posting requires UK residency"
  survives only as free text in `rejection_reason`, and only after the
  row was rejected. A row that is still open carries no eligibility
  information at all, so the candidate re-reads the posting to find out.
- **What eligibility signal exists is mixed into the fit score.** Spec
  078's blockers (US-only scope, W-2 and other US employment terms, US
  payroll) subtract a derived penalty from `fit_score`
  (`services/api/src/harrier/screening/rules.py`, `blockers`,
  `blocker_penalty`), and spec 081 applies the same floor to the learned
  score (`services/api/src/harrier/scoring/model.py`, `fit_score_for`).
  The only way to see "cannot hire here" is a negative number and a
  `blocker=` entry inside `signals`. Nothing marks the cases in between:
  a posting that names a route (contractor, employer of record) or says
  nothing about geography at all.
- **Region words are read as eligibility.** `PREFERRED_REGION_PATTERNS`
  gives the same bonus for `worldwide`, `global`, `anywhere`, `emea`,
  `europe`, `turkey` and `türkiye`. None of these alone says a person in
  Türkiye can be engaged. A posting located "EMEA" can still require UK
  right to work.
- **Restrictions are found late, by an LLM, and recorded as rejections.**
  The batch evaluator (spec 072) rejects postings whose text restricts
  residency, writing the finding as an `ai-evaluation:` rejection reason.
  The restriction becomes visible only when the row leaves the queue, the
  wording varies by run, and no excerpt or source URL is kept.

Who it hurts: the candidate, who spends review time on postings that
cannot hire them and cannot filter for the ones that explicitly can.

A read of the operator's own tracker export was done locally before this
spec was written. Its findings were reported in the session, not here
(ADR-008).

## Scope

The change adds one append-only table, one deterministic assessor on the
shared screening path, one write verb, and read and write surfaces on the
CLI and the API. It does not touch the fit score, the gates, the queue
order or the status lifecycle.

- `services/api/src/harrier/screening/eligibility.py` (new): the phrase
  tables, the assessor `assess_eligibility(job) -> Assessment`, and
  `eligibility_version()`.
- `services/api/src/harrier/screening/pipeline.py`: kept industry rows get
  a rule assessment, carried beside the row in `ScreenResult`.
- `services/api/src/harrier/discovery.py` and
  `services/api/src/harrier/capture.py`: pass each row's assessment to
  `add_job`, which writes it in the same transaction as the row.
- `services/api/src/harrier/tracker/schema.py`: migration 15, both
  dialects, creating `job_eligibility`; the table joins `OWNED_TABLES`
  and `GLOBAL_ID_TABLES`. Migrations 10 and 13 read their own frozen
  copies of those lists (`MIGRATION_10_OWNED_TABLES`,
  `MIGRATION_10_GLOBAL_ID_TABLES`), so adding a table rewrites no
  merged migration.
- `services/api/src/harrier/tracker/store.py`: `add_job` takes an
  optional first assessment; `record_eligibility` (the one other writer
  of the table) and `eligibility_of`, `eligibility_views`,
  `eligibility_history` (readers).
- `services/api/src/harrier/tracker/actions.py`: `set_eligibility` (the
  candidate's verdict) and `reassess_eligibility` (rule re-run on open
  rows).
- `services/api/src/harrier_cli/main.py`: `harrier eligibility show | set |
  reassess`, classed as database commands in `COMMAND_CLASSES`.
- `services/api/src/harrier_api/app.py`: `JobOut` gains `eligibility`;
  three routes (see API). `harrier_api/settings_routes.py` places the
  three commands on their routes in `COMMAND_PLACES`.
- `packages/contract/openapi.json` and `packages/contract/src/schema.d.ts`,
  regenerated by `just contract`. A guarded path, approved under this spec.
- Tests under `services/api/tests/`, synthetic postings and invented
  companies only. The two web test fixtures that build a `JobOut` by hand
  (`apps/web/src/entities/job/JobTable.test.tsx`,
  `apps/web/src/features/tracker/JobHistory.test.tsx`) gain the "no
  assessment" value, since the generated type now requires the field.

## Behavior

### The model

An **assessment** answers one question about one tracker row: can this
posting engage a person based in Türkiye, and through which route.

| Field | Values | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `verdict` | `confirmed`, `possible`, `unknown`, `excluded` | Geography only: whether the role may be done from Türkiye. See definitions below. |
| `routes` | a set drawn from `employee`, `contractor`, `eor`, `relocation`; empty reads as `unknown` | Engagement only: the hiring routes the evidence names. `contractor` covers direct contract, B2B and freelance. `eor` is an employer of record. |
| `evidence_url` | an `http` or `https` URL, or empty | Where the evidence was read. For a rule assessment, the row's `url`. |
| `excerpt` | text, at most 300 characters, or empty | The words that decided the verdict, as written in the source. |
| `field` | `title`, `location`, `description`, `external`, or empty | Where in the posting the excerpt came from. `external` is a candidate assessment citing a page other than the posting. |
| `matched` | `class:phrase` entries joined by `\|` | Every phrase that fired, so the verdict can be audited. Rule assessments only. |
| `checked_at` | UTC ISO-8601 timestamp | When the evidence was read. |
| `origin` | `rule` or `candidate` | Who made the assessment. |
| `version` | the eligibility version, or empty for `candidate` | Which phrase tables produced a rule assessment. |
| `description_sha256` | hex digest, or empty | The description the rule read, so a later reader can tell whether the text changed. Empty for `candidate`, and for a rule assessment made with no description. |

**Two dimensions, not one.** `verdict` says where the role permits work
from. `routes` says how the employer can engage the person. Neither is
derived from the other. A `confirmed` verdict says the posting names
Türkiye as a place the role can be done from; it does not say the
employer can put the person on Turkish payroll or accept a contractor.
A confirmed row's `routes` are only the routes whose own phrases fired,
and are empty (read `unknown`) when none did.

**Definitions.** These are conservative on purpose. Each verdict needs
evidence of its own kind; the absence of evidence is `unknown`.

- `confirmed`: the evidence names Türkiye as a place the role can be done
  from, and no restriction elsewhere in the posting contradicts it. It
  confirms the geography and nothing about the route.
- `possible`: the evidence names a route or a scope that could include
  Türkiye without saying it does. EU-permit, EU-residency and EU-entity
  phrases are `possible` (see the invariant below).
- `unknown`: nothing in the evidence speaks to it. This is the reading
  of every row with no assessment.
- `excluded`: an explicit restriction rules out Türkiye, and no route
  that would get around it (`eor`, `relocation`) is documented.

`worldwide`, `global`, `anywhere`, `remote`, `remote-first`, `EMEA` and
`Europe` alone are none of `confirmed`, `possible` or `excluded`. They
leave the verdict `unknown`.

**Per posting, not per company.** An assessment belongs to one tracker
row. Nothing in this spec copies a verdict from one row of a company to
another row of the same company, and nothing records a company-wide
policy. A candidate who knows of a company-wide policy records it on each
row it applies to, citing the page (`field = external`).

### The rule assessor

`assess_eligibility(job)` reads `title`, `location` and `description` of a
`NormalizedJob`. It is pure: no network, no LLM, no configuration beyond
its own tables. Text is folded Turkish-aware before matching (`İ` and `I`
to `i`, `ı` to `i`, combining dot removed), so `TÜRKİYE`, `Türkiye`,
`turkiye` and `Turkey` all match. The excerpt is cut from the original,
unfolded text.

Phrase classes (the tables in `eligibility.py` are the authority; this is
their shape, and every pattern is word-bounded or anchored):

| Class | Shape | Read in |
|---|---|---|
| `tr_named` | `turkey`, `türkiye`, `turkiye`, and Turkish cities (`istanbul`, `ankara`, `izmir`, `antalya`, `bursa`) | location, description |
| `restriction` | a requirement to be based in, reside in, live in, be located in, hold right to work in, or be a resident or citizen of a named place; "`<place>` only"; "remote (`<place>`)". `<place>` is a fixed table of countries and regions that do not include Türkiye and are not the EU, the EEA or Europe. | title, location, description |
| `us_restriction` | spec 078's `US_SCOPE_PATTERNS` and `EMPLOYMENT_BLOCKER_PATTERNS`, imported, not copied, with spec 078's EMEA-alternative rule and its location rule: a location naming an EMEA region (`rules.location_names_explicit_emea`) keeps a US scope phrase from firing, and nothing keeps an employment phrase from it | title, location, description |
| `eu_permit` | `rules.EU_PERMIT_PATTERNS`, imported, not copied | location, description |
| `reach` | "anywhere in the world", "from any country", "work from anywhere", "globally distributed" (the reach phrases of `US_PAYROLL_REACH_PATTERNS`) | location, description |
| `region` | `worldwide`, `global`, `anywhere`, `emea`, `europe`, `remote-first` | location, description |
| `route_contractor` | contractor, independent contractor, contract role, contract position, contract basis, B2B contract, B2B agreement, "on a B2B" basis, freelance, freelancer. Never bare "contract", so "employment contract" and "permanent contract" do not fire, and never bare "B2B", so "a B2B SaaS company" does not either. | title, location, description |
| `route_eor` | "employer of record", `eor`, "via" or "through" Deel, Remote.com or Oyster | description |
| `route_relocation` | relocation support, relocation package, relocation assistance, "we sponsor" visa or relocation | description |
| `route_employee` | full-time employee, permanent employee, employment contract, permanent position, permanent role | description |

A `restriction` or `us_restriction` phrase does not fire when its clause
offers Türkiye, EMEA or Europe as an alternative ("must be based in the
UK or Türkiye", "UK or EU"), by the same clause rule spec 078 applies in
`_offers_emea`. The rule is restated in `eligibility.py` with the folded
Türkiye forms added: 078's table matches `türkiye` only as written, and
its helper is private to `rules.py`.

A route phrase preceded in its own clause by a negation ("no relocation
support", "not a contract role", "we don't use an employer of record")
names no route.

**Verdict, first match wins:**

1. `confirmed` when `tr_named` fires in the location field and neither
   `restriction` nor `us_restriction` fires. The excerpt is the location.
2. `excluded` when `restriction` or `us_restriction` fires, `tr_named`
   does not fire anywhere, and neither `route_eor` nor `route_relocation`
   fires. The excerpt is the restriction's sentence.
3. `possible` when any of: `tr_named` fires in the description only;
   `eu_permit` fires; `reach` fires; `route_eor` fires; `route_relocation`
   fires; `route_contractor` and `region` both fire; or `tr_named` fires
   together with a restriction (a contradiction, left for the candidate).
   The excerpt is the first of these phrases in that order.
4. `unknown` otherwise. Excerpt empty.

`routes` is every route class that fired, whatever the verdict. The
verdict never adds a route and a route never raises the verdict to
`confirmed`: "EMEA" with "B2B contract" is `possible` with routes
`contractor`, because EMEA does not establish that Türkiye is covered.

### The invariant on EU phrases

EU-permit, EU-residency and EU-entity phrases ("EU work permit required",
"must be based in the EU", "EU-based contractor") are positive scoring
signals and never filters (CLAUDE.md, product invariants). In this spec
they produce `possible`, never `excluded` and never `confirmed`. They do
not move the fit score here; their existing weights in
`PREFERRED_SIGNAL_WEIGHTS` are unchanged.

### Where the rule assessment runs

On the industry kind only, in the shared screening path, after the gates
and scoring, for every row the pipeline keeps. The assessment is written
by `add_job` in the same transaction as the row. A source module never
assesses: this is screening, and sources are ingestion only.

The assessor never rejects a posting, never changes `fit_score`, `score`,
`signals` or `scoring_version`, and never reorders the queue. The gates,
the seen state and spec 078's blockers behave exactly as before. A
posting that is `excluded` is still in the tracker, at the rank its fit
score gives it.

Manual capture (`add_manually`, the bookmarklet) records a rule
assessment from whatever text it was given; with no description that is
usually `unknown`.

The academic kind gets no assessment. Its region scope is the `countries`
of its search entry (spec 097).

### The candidate's assessment

`set_eligibility(conn, scope, selector, verdict, routes, evidence_url,
excerpt, field, checked_at)` records an assessment with `origin =
candidate`.

- `confirmed` and `excluded` require a non-empty `evidence_url` and
  `excerpt`. `possible` and `unknown` accept them empty.
- `checked_at` defaults to now; a value in the future is refused.
- `field` defaults to `description` when `evidence_url` equals the row's
  `url`, and to `external` otherwise.

**The candidate's assessment is authoritative until the candidate
changes it.** The current assessment of a row is its latest `candidate`
assessment if it has one, and otherwise its latest `rule` assessment, and
otherwise `unknown` with `origin = none`. A later rule assessment never
replaces a candidate one as current, however it differs. It is still
recorded, with its evidence, its `version` and its `description_sha256`,
so the history shows what the rules said and on which text. Only another
`candidate` assessment changes a candidate verdict; the earlier one stays
in the history.

When the latest rule assessment of a row with a candidate assessment
differs from it in verdict or routes, the row reads as `disagrees` (see
the API). Nothing else follows from a disagreement: no status, rank or
current verdict changes.

`record_eligibility` refuses a rule assessment on a rejected row, so no
path assesses a rejection by rule.

Setting an eligibility verdict never changes the row's status, its
`rejection_reason`, its `next_action` or its `job_events`. Eligibility is
not a status. A candidate who wants to reject an `excluded` row does it
with the existing status verbs.

### Storage

`job_eligibility`, migration 15 on both dialects:

| Column | Type |
|---|---|
| `id` | integer primary key |
| `job_id` | references `jobs(id)`; on Postgres `(owner_id, job_id)` references `jobs(owner_id, id)` |
| `verdict` | text, CHECK in the four verdicts |
| `routes` | text, `\|`-joined route names in a fixed order, empty for none |
| `evidence_url`, `excerpt`, `field`, `matched` | text, default empty; `field` CHECK in its values or empty |
| `checked_at` | text, not null |
| `origin` | text, CHECK in `rule`, `candidate` |
| `version` | text, default empty |
| `description_sha256` | text, default empty |
| `at` | text, the time the row was written |

- Append-only by trigger on both dialects, as `job_events` is (spec 079).
  A correction is a new assessment. On Postgres, one owner's erasure
  removes that owner's rows, as it does for `job_events`. An update is
  never allowed, and `TRUNCATE` is refused. (Spec 111's `job_eligibility`
  amendment, approved 2026-10-11; not built yet.)
- Index on `(job_id, at)`.
- On Postgres: `owner_id` defaults to `auth.uid()`, the `owner_only`
  policy, row security enabled and forced, `id` drawn from the global
  sequence and not writable by `harrier_tenant` (spec 105, migrations 10
  and 13). The table joins `OWNED_TABLES` and `GLOBAL_ID_TABLES`, and
  `test_dialect_parity.py` holds both dialects to the same columns,
  defaults and refusals.

No column is added to `jobs`. The CSV export shape does not change.

### Versioning and reconsideration

`eligibility_version()` is a 12-hex sha256 over every table in
`eligibility.py`, the imported spec 078 and EU-permit tables, the fold,
and the verdict order. It is stored on each rule assessment.

It is **not** part of `policy_version` or `scoring_version`
(`services/api/src/harrier/screening/policy.py`). `policy_version`
describes screening decisions (spec 031), and the assessor makes none: it
decides no gate and no score. So a change to its tables must not make
seen rejections eligible for `harrier reconsider` or mark stored scores
stale (spec 033). Reconsideration is unchanged.

`reassess_eligibility(conn, scope, apply=False)` re-runs the assessor on
rows that meet all of:

- the row's status is not `rejected`;
- the row has a cached description (`load_cached_description`, as
  `rescore` uses);
- the row has no rule assessment, or its latest rule assessment differs
  from the current run in `version` or in `description_sha256`.

A row with a candidate assessment is reassessed like any other. The new
rule assessment is recorded and the candidate's stays current, as above.

It reports counts: `examined`, `assessed` (written, or would be without
`--apply`), `unchanged`, `under_candidate` (assessed rows whose current
verdict is the candidate's), `disagrees` (of those, how many now differ
from it), `rejected_skipped`, `no_description`, and the number per
verdict. Without `--apply` it writes nothing. A row whose new verdict
equals its old one still gets a new assessment under `--apply`, because
the version or the description changed and the history should say so.

### Backfill

None automatic. Migration 15 creates an empty table. Every existing row
reads `unknown`, `origin = none`, until the candidate sets it or runs
`harrier eligibility reassess --apply`. Nothing is inferred for a row
without its posting text, and no rejected row is ever assessed by rule,
so a human rejection is never revisited.

### CLI

- `harrier eligibility show <selector>`: the current assessment, then
  the history newest first. Exit 0; exit 1 on a selector that names no
  row or more than one, as every tracker verb.
- `harrier eligibility set <selector> --verdict V [--route R]...
  [--url U] [--excerpt TEXT] [--checked-at ISO]`: exit 0 and prints the
  new current assessment; exit 2 with a sentence naming the missing or
  invalid input; exit 1 on a selector that names no row.
- `harrier eligibility reassess [--apply]`: prints the report. Exit 0.

All three run on the default track only, as every verb outside
`NON_DEFAULT_OPERATIONS` does (specs 093, 094): any other track refuses
them, with exit 2 on the command line and a 409 over HTTP. Today every
other track is academic, because a second industry track is refused
(`harrier.tracks.kind_refusal`). The day one is allowed, these three join
`NON_DEFAULT_OPERATIONS` (post-merge review of PR #242).

### API

- `GET /jobs`: each `JobOut` gains `eligibility`: `verdict`, `routes`,
  `origin` (`rule`, `candidate`, `none`), `evidence_url`, `excerpt`,
  `field`, `checked_at`, `version`, `description_sha256`, and
  `disagrees` (true when the current assessment is the candidate's and
  the latest rule assessment differs from it in verdict or routes). A row
  with no assessment carries `verdict = unknown`, `origin = none`,
  `disagrees = false` and empty strings.
- `GET /tracker/{selector}/eligibility`: the history, newest first, each
  entry with its `matched` phrases and `at`.
- `POST /tracker/{selector}/eligibility`: body `verdict`, `routes`,
  `evidence_url`, `excerpt`, `checked_at` (optional). 200 with the new
  current assessment; 409 with the CLI's sentence for a missing or
  invalid input, and on any track but the default; 404 for an unknown
  selector.
  POST and 409, as every tracker write that names a job: spec 082's
  refusal table provokes each route's 404 and 409.
- `POST /ops/eligibility-reassess` with `apply`: the report, running the
  same function as the CLI (spec 095's pairing).

### Logging and privacy

- `job_eligibility` lives in the tracker database, never in git
  (ADR-002 as revised by ADR-008). It names no person. Its excerpts are
  posting text.
- No new path is created, so `config/data-classification.json` and
  `.gitignore` do not change. `data/` is already never-in-git.
- Excerpts and `matched` are never logged. The write path logs the job
  id, origin and verdict at debug. This spec adds nothing to run
  summaries or Telegram.
- Fixtures and tests use synthetic postings with invented companies.

## Failure modes

- **A restriction phrased in a way the table does not list** reads
  `unknown` or `possible`, not `excluded`. The candidate still sees the
  posting. No worse than today.
- **A `restriction` that fires on a posting the candidate can take**
  (a false positive) labels it `excluded` but does not hide, reject or
  reorder it. The excerpt shows why; the candidate overrides with `set`.
  The fix is a table edit, which moves the eligibility version, and
  `reassess --apply` updates rows that have no candidate verdict.
- **`tr_named` in a location that is an office, not a remote option**:
  hybrid and onsite postings are rejected by the location gate before
  assessment, so a kept posting naming Türkiye in its location passed
  as remote. A posting whose description then restricts to another
  country reads `possible`, not `confirmed`.
- **Company names that look like routes** (Deel, Remote.com, Oyster):
  `route_eor` reads only the description, and only with "via" or
  "through", so a posting from those companies is not marked `eor` by
  its company field.
- **A candidate assessment followed by a later discovery run** of the
  same posting: the tracker dedupe gate drops the posting before
  assessment, so discovery writes nothing. Only `reassess` adds rule
  assessments to such a row, and they never become current.
- **A rule that changes its mind about a row the candidate decided**:
  the rule assessment is recorded and the row reads `disagrees = true`.
  The candidate's verdict stays current until the candidate records
  another.
- **Concurrent writers**: `record_eligibility` runs inside the caller's
  transaction. Two writers on one row produce two history entries; the
  later `at` is current within its origin.
- **A database at migration 14 opened by this code**: migration 15 runs
  as every migration does, whole or not at all (spec 090).
- Must not introduce: a gate verdict or a rejection that depends on
  eligibility; a change to `fit_score` or the learned features; an
  EU-permit phrase read as `excluded`; a rule assessment becoming current
  over a candidate one; a rule assessment on a rejected row; a `confirmed`
  verdict from `worldwide`, `global`, `anywhere`, `emea` or `europe`; a
  `confirmed` verdict that sets or implies a route.

## Proof / origin

  is new behavior.
- The EU-phrase rule is the product invariant in `CLAUDE.md` ("Remote-only
  and EMEA scope are enforced on the industry kind"), pinned today by
  `services/api/tests/test_scoring.py::test_eu_permit_phrases_are_never_blockers`.
- The US restriction tables and the clause rule for alternatives come from
  spec 078 (`services/api/src/harrier/screening/rules.py`, `blockers`,
  `_offers_emea`).
- The append-only shape and the actor precedence follow spec 079's
  `job_events` (`services/api/src/harrier/tracker/schema.py`, migration 7).
- Owner policing follows spec 105 (`OWNED_TABLES`, `GLOBAL_ID_TABLES`).
- Keeping the version out of `policy_version` follows from spec 031's
  contract: `policy_version` stamps gate decisions, and this assessor makes
  none.
- "Flag with evidence; never reject or reorder" is the precedent of spec
  097's academic flags.

## Out of scope

- **The browser surface.** A badge in the tracker table, the evidence in
  the detail row, a filter by verdict and a form for `set` and
  `reassess`. A follow-up spec will propose it now that this spec's model
  is agreed. Until then the API routes exist and the browser does not
  call them, a gap against spec 095 that the follow-up closes.
- **Queue order, digest and Telegram.** Deferred, not open. No verdict
  changes rank or what the digest lists, consistent with spec 097's flags
  ("A flag never rejects and never reorders"). Whether `excluded` should
  rank last is a later spec's decision, made with this spec's data in
  hand.
- **Spec 078 and 081 blockers.** They keep subtracting from `fit_score`.
  Moving them out of the score into eligibility changes a shipped spec
  and is not done here.
- **The learned feature `explicit_emea_remote`**, which reads the
  location's region into the learned score. Unchanged here.
- **Company-wide eligibility, and a company watchlist.** No company
  table, no inheritance between rows of one company. The ATS board list
  stays the `feeds` kind of spec 023.
- **LinkedIn saved searches.** They are operator configuration in
  `user_config`, not behavior, and no search URL is recorded on a row.
- **The batch evaluator (spec 072)**, which may still reject on a
  residency finding. Its prompt and its rejections are unchanged.
- **LLM assessment.** The assessor is phrase tables only.
- **The academic kind.**
- **Screening's own Turkish case folding** in `rules.normalize`. The
  assessor folds for itself; the gates are not changed.

## Migration

Migration 15 runs on the next start of the API or CLI, on both stores.
Nothing else is required. To give open rows a verdict from their cached
descriptions, the operator runs, once:

```
harrier eligibility reassess
harrier eligibility reassess --apply
```

## Amendments in the implementing change

Found while implementing, and amended here rather than left to disagree
with the code:

1. **Migration 15, not 14.** Spec 105's second post-merge review took 14
   on main before this landed.
2. **Migrations 10 and 13 read frozen lists.** Both iterate
   `OWNED_TABLES` and `GLOBAL_ID_TABLES`; adding a table to either would
   have rewritten merged history. They now read
   `MIGRATION_10_OWNED_TABLES` and `MIGRATION_10_GLOBAL_ID_TABLES`, as
   migration 1 reads `_ORIGINAL_NOTE_KEYS`.
3. **The write is `add_job`'s.** There is no `add_prospects`; discovery
   and capture call `add_job` per row, which now takes the row's first
   assessment and writes both in one transaction.
4. **POST and 409, not PUT and 400.** Every tracker write that names a
   job is a POST whose refusals are 404 and 409 (spec 082's table).
5. **Exit 1 on a selector that names no row**, as every tracker verb.
6. **Bare "B2B" is not a route.** "A B2B SaaS company" describes the
   business. The contractor table names B2B with a contract word.
7. **A negated route names no route**, and the US scope phrases keep spec
   078's location rule as well as its alternative rule.
8. **Nothing is added to run summaries or Telegram.** "Counts per verdict
   only" read as a promise of new output; it was meant as a limit.
9. **`record_eligibility` refuses a rule assessment on a rejected row**,
   so the property holds on every path, not only in `reassess`.

## Decided in review (2026-10-11)

1. **Geography and route are separate dimensions.** The rule may say
   `confirmed` from a location naming Türkiye, and that confirms the
   geography only, never a route ("Two dimensions, not one").
2. **"EMEA" with a contractor route is `possible`**, never `confirmed`:
   EMEA does not establish that Türkiye is covered.
3. **Queue order is deferred**, not open (Out of scope).
4. **A candidate verdict is authoritative until the candidate changes
   it.** Later rule assessments are still recorded with their evidence,
   version and description hash, and a difference reads as `disagrees`.
